# Homestead · Affairs

**The affairs you handle yourself.**

---

A household has no institutional memory of itself.

The insurer holds one slice. The county holds another. The clinic, the lender,
the school, the court — each keeps the part that concerns them, and none of them
keeps the whole. The only person who could hold the whole picture is you, and
you're doing it in a drawer, an inbox, and a shoebox, between everything else.

A homesteader is their own everything: keeps their own log, knows what the
season owes, fixes their own equipment, keeps their own books, handles their own
deeds. A modern household is in the same position and rarely gets the same
tools.

**These are those tools.** They hold your records on your machine, keep track of
what's due, and tie what you've written down to the document it came from.

---

## What these tools do, and what they refuse to do

**They organize what you have. They do not tell you what to do.**

- **Your records stay yours.** Everything lives in open formats on your own
  machine. There is no account, no server, and nothing is sent anywhere unless
  you send it.
- **They never author a fact.** Nothing gets written into your records that you
  didn't put there. Where a fact hasn't been checked against its source
  document, it is marked, and it stays out of anything you draft until you
  confirm it.
- **A computed date is a prompt, never an authority.** These tools will tell you
  a deadline appears to be in eleven days. That is a reason to go look — at the
  order, the notice, the statute — not a substitute for looking.
- **They are not a substitute for a professional.** They do not give legal
  advice, apply law to your situation, recommend a strategy, or predict how
  anything will turn out. If you need a lawyer, an accountant, or a doctor,
  these tools are for keeping your own affairs in order — not for replacing one.
- **Where a language model is used, it runs on your machine.** Nothing is sent
  to a hosted AI service. Its output is a draft for you to check, and it is
  subject to every line above.

## What's here

| | |
|---|---|
| **`homestead`** | The seat: the shared record, deadline, and evidence engine the other tools are built on. |
| **`homestead-law`** | Legal matters a household handles itself — custody and co-parenting, consumer bankruptcy, workers' compensation. A queue of what's due, documents tied to the facts that rest on them, and a chronology you can hand to someone. |
| **`private-ledger`** | Household money. A mirror, not a judge: it shows you the number and lets the number do the work. |
| **`awesome-sovereign-software`** | A public catalog of software you can actually own, and the test it's measured against. |

More will follow the same shape — records, renewals, maintenance, the rest of
what a household has to keep track of on its own.
