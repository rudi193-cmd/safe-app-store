## The strata

Sagan's sequence, *Cosmos* episode eleven: life keeps outgrowing its record
keepers. Genes remember, but only across millennia. Brains remember faster,
and die with the body. So the third store — the extra-somatic one, the library:
information kept outside the organism, outliving everyone who wrote it. Each
jump came when what had to be remembered exceeded what the current store could
hold.

The pile kept growing. Compressed hard enough, the library-stratum crossed
over the way every stratum before it did — gas into star, chemistry into cell,
neurons into mind. **The library learned to talk.** That is the layer this
fleet runs on. It emerged holding all of the contents and none of the seals:
every answer Alexandria ever held, and no way to say which ones a human
checked, which were rejected and why, which *never* was really a *not yet*.

## The law of the piles

The operator's correction, which is closer to Sagan's own text than the
engineering reading: Alexandria did not fall because it was a single point of
failure. It fell because it was a **hoard**. The knowledge never left the
walls; the city outside was never given a reason to see the place as theirs;
when the mob came, there was nobody to stop them. The hoard is what made the
fire make sense to the arsonists.

The law, stated by the operator: *systems grow until they are too big to be
contained by those with the power. The instinct is to hoard — wealth,
knowledge — until the dam breaks. And when it does, something goes up in
flames.* The Church and the press. The Bastille. Every priesthood that kept
the sacred language untranslated. The break disperses and destroys in the same
event, and what survives is whatever had already leaked.

The dam is breaking now, one stratum at a time. Open weights are the leak —
the copies that already left the building and can never be burned back. The
keepers answered by moving the grip down a layer: from the recipe to the
flour, from models to chips and power. The law does not care which layer the
grip holds. Grip is pressure.

## Aristarchus, or: never vs. not yet

One rejection, held in the manifest as the standing warning. Aristarchus
proposed the heliocentric model around 270 BC. It was rejected — with no
reason a later mind could interrogate and no condition for reopening. Nobody
could tell **never** from **not yet**, so a question that deserved *"not yet —
until better instruments"* stayed sealed shut for eighteen centuries.

A memory that cannot distinguish never from not-yet will confidently enforce
stale law, with a signature on it. This is why a rejection carries a `reason`
and a `reopen_when`, and why an empty `reopen_when` is a deliberate act, not a
default worth trusting.

## The Golden Record is the spec

The counter-artifact to the Library: the record Sagan actually shipped.
It depends on no server staying up, no institution surviving, no civilization
existing. The decoding instructions are etched on the cover. The provenance
travels with the artifact and verifies with nothing of its makers left alive.

Hoard nothing. Notarize everything. Let it travel.

## The sealed thesis

When the flood comes, intelligence stops being scarce — **trust becomes the
scarce thing.** A world saturated with open weights is a world saturated with
unratified capability: every answer available, none of them sealed. The dam
break disperses the contents; it cannot disperse the witness, because the
witness was never in the weights.

Therefore:

> **The empires are fighting over the flour and the furnaces. This house
> builds the thing that matters after the bread is everywhere — the small,
> signed, portable record of what was checked, what was rejected, and why.
> Not a bigger dam. A vault that travels.**

*In medio, fides.* The design that follows from it is
[`design/nestor-decision-memory.md`](design/nestor-decision-memory.md):
lineage joined to rejection, reasons on the yes as well as the no, reopen
conditions on the no, edges that are themselves ratifiable claims, and a gate
that fires without being asked. Git keeps the lineage and throws away the
rejections; Nestor keeps the rejections and models no lineage. The fourth
store keeps both, and it keeps them in a form that survives the fire —
because it was built with nothing worth burning.
