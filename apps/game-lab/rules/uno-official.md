# Uno — Official Rules, the True Gaps, and Our House Deviations
*Corrected. The official baseline was fetched and cited this time; every house choice is tagged so nothing is disguised as official. ΔΣ=42.*

## Part A — Official rules (Mattel), verified
- **Deck:** classic **108 cards** — for each of 4 colors: one 0, two each of 1–9, two each of Skip / Reverse / Draw Two; plus 4 Wild and 4 Wild Draw Four. (Modern retail decks add Wild Swap Hands, Wild Shuffle Hands, and 3 blank customizables → 112.)
- **Deal 7** each; flip one to start the discard pile. If it's a Wild Draw Four, return it and reflip.
- **A turn:** play a card matching the top by **color, number, or symbol**, or a Wild. **Can't play → draw one; if it's playable you may play it, otherwise your turn ends.**
- **Skip** — next player loses their turn. **Reverse** — flip direction (acts as a Skip in a 2-player game). **Draw Two** — next player draws 2 and is skipped. **Wild** — player names the new color.
- **Wild Draw Four (restricted):** may **only** be played when you hold **no card matching the current color** (you may still hold a matching number or a Wild). Next player draws 4 and is skipped.
- **Challenge:** the next player may challenge a Wild Draw Four. Look at the hand — played **illegally → the player draws 4**; challenge **wrong → the challenger draws 6.**
- **"Uno":** at one card you must call it; **caught silent before the next player acts → draw 2.**
- **Scoring (winner takes points in others' hands):** numbers = face value; Skip / Reverse / Draw Two = **20**; Wild / Wild Draw Four = **50** (Swap/Shuffle Hands = 40 where present). **First to 500 points wins the match.**
- **Mattel's official ruling: stacking is NOT allowed** — a Draw Two/Four must be drawn immediately; you cannot pass the penalty on with another draw card.

## Part B — The genuinely-open gaps (official rules are silent)
These are the real gaps a table must settle, because Mattel doesn't:
1. **A Draw Two/Four flipped as the very first up-card** — most instruction sheets say the first player draws and is skipped, but editions differ; agree before dealing.
2. **Two players catching a missed "Uno" at once** — who gets to call it / whether both penalties apply.
3. **Winning on a Draw Two/Four** — the hand ends when you empty your hand; whether the pending draw still applies to the next player is unstated (house call).
4. **Running the draw pile completely dry when the discard can't be reshuffled fast enough** — reshuffle timing/what if truly out.
5. **Must you announce a color instantly on a Wild, and can you change it** — no official timing rule.

## Part C — Our house set for the game that was played (each tagged)
- **Stacking +2/+4 — [HOUSE · OVERRIDES OFFICIAL].** Official bans it; our table ran Progressive-style stacking, drawer-eats-the-pile-if-they-can't-continue.
- **Wild Draw Four playable anytime, no challenge — [HOUSE · OVERRIDES OFFICIAL].** Official restricts it to "no matching color" and allows the draw-4 / draw-6 challenge; our table dropped both for pace.
- **7-0 rule (7 swaps hands, 0 rotates all hands) — [HOUSE · not official].**
- **Jump-in — [HOUSE · not official]** — and voted OUT at our table anyway.
- **Draw one, play if able else pass — [OFFICIAL].**
- **"Uno" penalty = draw 2 if caught — [OFFICIAL].**
- **Scoring values; first to 500 — [OFFICIAL].**

> Bottom line: the game I ran (`THE_UNO_GAME.md`) was a valid **house / Progressive Uno** game, but three of its rules deviate from official Mattel play — stacking, the unrestricted un-challengeable Wild Draw Four, and 7-0. It is **not** a game of official Uno. My earlier writeup failed to establish this baseline and mislabeled Mattel-ruled points as open gaps; this document is the correction.
