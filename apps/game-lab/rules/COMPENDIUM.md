# Part I — Board Classics

## Backgammon

### Board and the 24 Points
Four quadrants of six triangles ("points") each = 24 points: your home board, your outer board, your opponent's outer board, your opponent's home board. A raised strip, the **bar**, divides the halves. Points are numbered 1–24 from each player's own perspective — point 1 is deepest in your home board, point 24 deepest in the opponent's; the numbering mirrors for each player.

### Starting Position
15 checkers each (own numbering): **2 on the 24-point, 5 on the 13-point, 3 on the 8-point, 5 on the 6-point.** Symmetric and mirrored.

### Object
Move all 15 checkers into your home board (points 1–6), then **bear them off**. First to bear off all 15 wins.

### Direction
Each player moves from higher-numbered points toward their own 1-point (and off) — so the two move in opposite physical directions.

### Rolling and Moving
- **Opening:** each rolls one die; higher moves first, playing *both* numbers just rolled. Tie → re-roll.
- Thereafter each player rolls two dice. The two numbers are two separate moves — two checkers moving one die each, or one checker moving the sum, provided the intermediate point (reached by the first die) is open.
- **Doubles**: play the number **four times** (5-5 = four moves of 5), across one to four checkers.
- A checker may only move to an **open point** (not held by 2+ enemy checkers).
- **Must use both dice** if legally possible (all four on a double). If only one can be played, play it; if either single but not both, play the **larger**. If neither can be played, forfeit the turn. You may not decline a legal move to gain advantage — play the maximum legally available.

### Hitting and the Bar
- A point with exactly one checker is a **blot**. An opposing checker landing there **hits** it; the blot goes to the **bar**. You may never land on a point held by 2+ enemy checkers.

### Entering from the Bar
- Checkers on the bar must **re-enter in the opponent's home board before any other move**, on a point matching a die (unless held by 2+ enemies). If neither die's entry point is open, forfeit the turn. Multiple checkers on the bar each enter on a separate die; only after all have entered may leftover numbers be used elsewhere.

### Bearing Off
- Begins only when **all 15 checkers are in your home board** (1–6). If hit during bear-off, that checker re-enters at the opponent's home and travels all the way around again before you may resume.
- Bear a checker off by rolling the number matching its point.
- **If the exact number can't be filled:** if a checker sits on a **higher** point than the die, you may not bear off — you must move within the home board instead. If **no checker is on a higher** point, you must bear off from the highest occupied point using that die.
- First to bear off all 15 wins.

### The Doubling Cube
- Faces 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64; starts centered "on 1," owned by neither. Before rolling on your turn (if you own the cube or it's centered), you may **propose doubling**. Opponent either **refuses** (concedes, pays the pre-double stake) or **accepts** (takes the cube at the new value; only the new owner may later redouble). Can escalate several times per game.

### Gammon and Backgammon Scoring
Final value = cube value × multiplier: **single** (loser bore off ≥1 checker) = 1×; **gammon** (loser bore off none) = 2×; **backgammon** (loser bore off none *and* still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board) = 3×.

### Crawford Rule (optional, match play)
When a player first reaches one point short of the match, the next game (the "Crawford game") is played **with no doubling cube**; normal doubling resumes after. Optional convention, not a universal law.

*Sources: [bkgm.com/rules](https://www.bkgm.com/rules.html) · [Wikipedia: Backgammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backgammon) · [USBGF rules](https://usbgf.org/backgammon-basics-how-to-play/)*

## Checkers (Draughts)

### Board & Setup
8×8 board, play only on the **32 dark squares**, a dark square in each player's near-left corner. **12 men each**, on the dark squares of the three rows nearest each player. The far row (opponent's side) is your **king row**.

### Movement
Men move **one square diagonally forward** to an empty dark square; never backward while uncrowned. Play alternates.

### Forced Captures (Mandatory Jump)
- Jump diagonally over an adjacent enemy into the empty square beyond, removing it. **Jumping is mandatory** — if any legal jump exists you must take one. Men jump **forward only**; only kings jump backward.
- The old "huffing" penalty is **obsolete**; a missed mandatory jump is simply corrected.

### Multiple Jumps
- If the same piece can jump again after landing, it **must continue**, chaining all available captures in that turn. If multiple different capture sequences exist, in American/English rules you may **choose any legal one** (no maximum-capture requirement, unlike International).

### Kings
- A man reaching the opponent's king row is **crowned** (stack a second piece). The move ends there — no further jump the same turn even if one is available. **Kings move and capture diagonally forward or backward, one square at a time** (no flying kings in American/English draughts).

### Winning & Drawing
- **Win** by capturing all enemy pieces or leaving the opponent with no legal move. **Draw** by agreement, or when neither can force a win (tournaments use e.g. a 40-move rule).

> GAP: Variant differences compressed. **International (Polish) draughts** — 10×10, 20 pieces, **flying kings** (slide any distance), and **maximum-capture** required. **Backward capture by men** differs by variant: American/English = kings only; International/Russian/Turkish = men may capture backward. Russian/Turkish have further distinct rules (Turkish uses orthogonal movement). Full variant rulesets not carried here.

*Sources: [Wikipedia: English Draughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_draughts) · [Wikipedia: Draughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draughts)*

## Chess

### Board & Setup
8×8, 64 squares, **white square in each player's near-right corner** ("white on right"). 16 pieces each. Back rank, corner inward: **Rook – Knight – Bishop – Queen – King – Bishop – Knight – Rook.** The **queen starts on her own color** (White queen d1 light, Black queen d8 dark); king takes e1/e8. Pawns fill the second rank. White moves first; alternate turns.

### Movement
- **King** — one square any direction; may never move into or stay in check.
- **Queen** — any distance straight or diagonal.
- **Rook** — any distance along rank or file.
- **Bishop** — any distance diagonally (bound to its start color).
- **Knight** — an "L" (2 then 1 perpendicular); the only piece that **jumps** over others.
- **Pawn** — one square straight forward to an empty square; **two squares on its first move** (both empty); **captures one square diagonally forward** only; never backward.
All but the knight are blocked by any piece in the path.

### Special move 1: Castling
King moves two squares toward a rook; that rook jumps to the square the king crossed. **Permanently forbidden** if the king has moved, or that rook has moved (even if returned). **Temporarily forbidden** if any piece stands between them, the king is in check, the king would pass over an attacked square, or would land on an attacked square. Only the king's squares matter — it's legal even if the rook is attacked or its transit square is attacked.

### Special move 2: En passant
If an enemy pawn advances **two squares** and lands directly beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it *as if it had moved one square* — moving diagonally onto the skipped square, removing the enemy pawn. **Only on the very next move**; the right is lost otherwise.

### Special move 3: Promotion
A pawn reaching the far rank **must** immediately become a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of its color (player's choice) — **not limited to captured pieces** (you can have multiple queens). Cannot stay a pawn or become a king.

### Check, Checkmate, Draws
- **Check:** king under attack; you must escape immediately — move the king, capture the attacker, or block (blocking impossible vs a knight or adjacent checker).
- **Checkmate:** in check with no legal escape → the checkmaker wins. Also won by resignation (or, timed, opponent's flag falls with mating material present).
- **Draws:** **Stalemate** (not in check but no legal move — a draw, *not* a loss). **Dead position / insufficient material** (K v K; K+B v K; K+N v K; K+B v K+B same color) — automatic. **Threefold repetition** (same position, same player to move, same rights, third time — claimed; automatic at fivefold). **Fifty-move rule** (50 moves each with no pawn move or capture — claimed; automatic at 75). **Agreement** (after ≥1 move).

> GAP: **Touch-move & clock/tournament rules** (casual essentials only): touch your piece → must move it if legal; touch an opponent's → must capture if legal; say "j'adoube/I adjust" first to rearrange. Timed: flag fall loses unless the opponent has insufficient mating material (then draw). Illegal moves are retracted with penalties. Full arbiter procedure, notation, and time-control specifics live in the FIDE Laws, Articles 6–8.

*Sources: [FIDE Laws of Chess 2023](https://rcc.fide.com/fide-laws-of-chess_fulltexthtml/) · [Wikipedia: Rules of chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_chess)*

# Part II — Bluffing & Social Deduction

## Liar's Dice (Perudo)
**Players:** 2–6. **Kit:** 5 dice + an opaque cup each. **Setup:** shake, slam upside-down, peek at your own dice only.
**Loop:** clockwise. A bid states a **quantity + face** — "five fours" — claiming at least that many of that face across **all** dice on the table. Each player must **raise** (higher quantity, or higher face at same quantity, or both) or **challenge**.
**Aces wild:** ones count as any face (except in Palifico). To switch a bid **to** aces, quantity ≈ half the previous (round up); to switch **away** from aces, at least double the aces quantity + 1.
**Challenge ("Dudo"/"Liar"):** reveal all dice, count (incl. wild aces). Bid met/exceeded → **challenger loses a die**; bid short → **bidder loses a die**. Loser of the die opens next.
**Calza / spot-on:** claim the bid is *exactly* right → if exact, caller **regains** a die; if off either way, caller **loses** a die. (Usually barred at two players; edition-dependent in Palifico.)
**Palifico:** when a player is down to one die, the next round aces are **not** wild, and after that player opens, only the **quantity** may be raised — the face is locked.
**Win:** lose your last die = out; last player standing wins.
> Edge disputes: Calza-in-Palifico and the exact aces halving/doubling rounding vary by edition (Perudo vs Dudo vs Cacho).
*Sources: [Wikipedia: Liar's dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_dice) · [Perudo rulebook](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/f4/4f/09-perudo-rulebook.pdf)*

## Coup
**Players:** 3–6. **Kit:** 15 cards (3 each of Duke, Assassin, Captain, Ambassador, Contessa); coins.
**Setup:** deal **2 face-down "influence"** cards each (your hidden identities); 2 coins each.
**Actions (one per turn):** **Income** (+1, unblockable); **Foreign Aid** (+2, blocked by a claimed Duke); **Coup** (pay 7, target loses an influence — unblockable/unchallengeable; **mandatory if you start your turn with 10+ coins**); **Duke – Tax** (+3, blocks Foreign Aid); **Assassin – Assassinate** (pay 3, target loses influence, blocked only by Contessa — the 3 coins are spent even if blocked); **Captain – Steal** (+2 from a player, blocked by Ambassador or Captain); **Ambassador – Exchange** (draw 2, keep/swap, return the rest; blocks stealing); **Contessa** (no action; blocks assassination).
**Bluff & challenge:** any character action or block may be **challenged**. Challenged player reveals — if they **have** the card, the challenger loses an influence and the revealer reshuffles + redraws; if they **don't**, the bluffer loses an influence and the action/block fails.
**Losing / winning:** a revealed lost influence is face-up and out; lose both = eliminated. Last player with influence wins.
*Sources: [Wikipedia: Coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game)) · [Coup rulebook PDF](https://artofthegame.github.io/coup/rulebook.pdf)*

## Skull
**Players:** 3–6. **Kit:** a mat + 4 discs each (3 roses, 1 skull).
**Loop:** on your turn either **place** a card face-down on your own stack, or (once everyone has placed at least one) **start bidding**.
**Bidding:** name a number of cards you'll flip face-up (from any stacks) without hitting a skull; others raise or pass; last one in becomes the **Challenger**.
**Reveal:** the Challenger must flip **all of their own cards first**, then may flip opponents' (their choice of stack, top down). Reach your bid in roses → **win a point**; flip any **skull** → lose the challenge.
**Skull penalty:** on a skull, discard one of your own cards permanently; 0 cards = eliminated.
**Win:** first to **two** successful challenges (or last player remaining).
> Most-argued rule: you *must* clear your own stack first, even if you know it dooms you.
*Sources: [Wikipedia: Skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull_(card_game))*

## Cheat (a.k.a. Bullshit / I Doubt It)
**Players:** ~2–10+. **Kit:** one 52-card deck (two for big groups), dealt out evenly.
**Loop:** clockwise, ranks are called in **strict ascending order** around the table (Aces, then Twos, then Threes… wrapping). On your turn play one or more cards **face-down** while announcing the mandated rank — **you need not actually hold it** (you may lie).
**Challenge ("Cheat!"):** any player may challenge the last play. Reveal: if the player **lied**, they take the **whole pile**; if they were **truthful**, the challenger takes the pile. Only the most recent play is challengeable (once the next player has played, it's too late).
**Win:** first to empty your hand — and survive any challenge on your final play.
*Sources: [Wikipedia: Cheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheat_(game)) · [Pagat: Cheat](https://www.pagat.com/beating/cheat.html)*

## Werewolf (Mafia)
**Players:** 5+ (best 8–18). **Kit:** role cards; a non-playing **Moderator**.
**Roles:** **Werewolves/Mafia** (informed minority, ~1 in 4–5, know each other, kill one per night); **Villagers** (no power, must deduce); **Seer/Detective** (learns one player's alignment each night); optional **Doctor/Bodyguard**, **Witch**, **Hunter**, etc.
**Loop:** **Night** — all close eyes; the Moderator wakes roles in sequence to act silently (werewolves pick a victim, seer investigates, doctor protects). **Day** — the Moderator announces the night's victim (eliminated, usually role revealed); survivors discuss, accuse, and **vote** to lynch one. Repeat.
**Win:** **Villagers** win when all werewolves are gone; **Werewolves** win at parity (equal to / outnumbering the rest).
> Disputes: exact werewolf win threshold (equal vs outnumber), tie-vote handling, and night-action order all vary by house rules.
*Sources: [Wikipedia: Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game)) · [Wikipedia: Are You a Werewolf?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_You_a_Werewolf%3F)*

## Two Truths and a Lie
**Players:** any (4+). **Kit:** none — purely verbal.
**Loop:** on your turn, state **three statements about yourself** — two true, one false — in any order, straight-faced. The group discusses and **guesses which is the lie**; you then reveal. No universal win condition (optional: point per correct guess, or per person fooled).
*Sources: [officialgamerules.org: Two Truths and a Lie](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/two-truths-and-a-lie/)*

# Part III — Roleplaying Game

## Dungeons & Dragons 5e (the general shape)
A DM narrates a shared fantasy world; players run characters; dice resolve uncertainty.

- **The core d20 test.** Almost everything: roll a d20, add the relevant **ability modifier** (+ **proficiency bonus** if trained), compare to a **Difficulty Class (DC)** — or to a target's **Armor Class** for attacks. Meet or beat = success.
- **Ability scores & modifiers.** Six abilities — STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA — usually 8–15 at level 1, up to 20. Modifier = `floor((score − 10) / 2)` (10–11 → +0, 16–17 → +3).
- **Advantage / disadvantage.** Roll two d20s, take higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage); they cancel if both apply.
- **AC** = number an attack must meet/beat. **HP** = damage taken before dropping to 0. **Saving throws** = d20 tests to resist effects. **Skill checks** = d20 tied to an ability (Stealth=DEX, Persuasion=CHA…).
- **Combat round (6 seconds).** Roll **initiative** (d20 + DEX) for turn order. Each turn: one **action**, maybe one **bonus action**, **movement** up to speed, and one **reaction** per round (often on others' turns). **Attack** = d20 + modifier + proficiency vs AC; on a hit, roll damage dice + modifier.
- **Spellcasting.** Limited **spell slots** by level (cantrips free); **spell save DC** = 8 + proficiency + casting modifier; some spells need **concentration** (one at a time; damage forces a CON save, DC 10 or half the damage).
- **Character creation shape.** Species/race + class (Fighter, Wizard, Cleric…) + background. Advance **level 1→20**, gaining HP, features, spells. **Proficiency bonus** = +2 (levels 1–4), rising +1 every four levels to +6 (17–20).
- **Rests.** Short rest (~1 hr): spend Hit Dice, recover some features. Long rest (~8 hr): full HP, all slots, most abilities.

> GAP: This is the shape, not the whole game. Every class/subclass, the full spell list, monster stat blocks, magic items, and the exception-based edge cases live in the *Player's Handbook / DMG / Monster Manual* and the free **System Reference Document**, released under **CC-BY-4.0** — SRD 5.2.1: https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf · landing: https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd

*Sources: [D&D Beyond SRD](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd)*

# Part IV — Make-Believe & Improv

## Bluey — the kids' games
The Heeler kids invent games with improvised "rulebooks," enforced with total sincerity even as the rules mutate mid-play.

- **Keepy Uppy** — keep a balloon off the ground as long as possible; players add obstacles (a fan, a leaf blower); ends when it finally lands/pops.
- **Shadowlands** — sunlight is deadly "water/crocodiles"; only shade is safe. Cross the yard shadow to shadow (even waiting for a cloud's shadow), no cutting corners — *"games are more fun if you follow the rules."*
- **Grannies** — each child plays an old-lady persona (Grannie Rita, Grannie Janet) with very specific rules (e.g., "grannies don't floss" — no floss dance).
- **Featherwand** — a wand casts pretend spells: an object becomes "impossibly heavy," or a person is turned into an animal/object and must act it.
- **Magic Xylophone** — a mallet freezes/unfreezes a person with a "ding"; meant to be shared by turns (the conflict is hogging the mallet).
- **The Claw** — one player is a claw-machine claw deciding whether others "win" a prize.
- **Taxi** — chairs as a car; roles (driver, passenger, mechanic, satnav); unwritten good-service rules exist mainly to be broken for chaos.
- **Shops / Markets** — a pretend stall with roles and pretend money; much of the "game" is negotiating the setup.
- **Squash** — two kids "controllers" steer two adults through a real game of squash, Pong-style.
- **Camping** — a tent/sleeping bags for pretend outdoors and stargazing.
- Others: **Chattermax**, **Rug Island** (cushions as islands, floor = lava/sea), **Hospital**, **The Adventure** (treasure map through the house), **Restaurant/Hotel**, **Daddy Robot**, **Bike**, **Horsey Ride**, **Dance Mode**, **Bad Mood** (parent as a mech piloted by the child).

> GAP: These are children's make-believe, not published rulesets — each "ruleset" is one episode's snapshot. In the show's ethos, rules are invented on the spot, treated as **sacred in the moment** (Bluey insists everyone "follow the rules"), yet freely renegotiated, added to, or broken next time **as long as everyone agrees**. No canonical fixed version exists. *(Note: the Bluey wiki's aggregate "List of Games" index returned a 402 on fetch, so this was assembled from individual game pages + cross-checked third-party roundups.)*

*Sources: [Bluey Wiki](https://blueypedia.fandom.com/wiki/Shadowlands) · [In The Playroom](https://intheplayroom.co.uk/bluey-games-from-the-show/) · [Fatherly: 19 Bluey games](https://www.fatherly.com/entertainment/bluey-indoor-games-rules-keepy-uppy)*

## Improv games
Short-form formats are lightweight constraints that structure a scene from suggestions.

- **Freeze (Freeze Tag)** — two play a scene; someone yells "Freeze!"; positions hold, one is tagged out, the new player starts a *different* scene justified by the same pose.
- **Two-Headed Expert** — two performers as one "expert," alternating one word at a time.
- **Questions Only** — a scene entirely in questions; a statement/hesitation/repeat gets you out.
- **World's Worst** — a suggested role; performers step up for rapid one-line "worst possible" jokes.
- **Hitchhiker** — mimed car; a hitchhiker with a strong quirk boards; when the driver picks up the quirk, everyone shifts and a new quirk boards.
- **Party Quirks** — a host guesses each guest's secretly-assigned quirk as they escalate clues.
- **Emotional Rollercoaster** — a conductor calls out new emotions the players must instantly shift into.
- **"Roulette"-style** — a scene built from independently-randomized **character + situation + emotion/genre** (cards, suggestions, or a generator), then played out. *(This is the shape of the "improv roulette" we ran earlier.)*

> GAP: Improv has no fixed rulebook or governing body — these are conventions from practice (rooted in Viola Spolin, popularized by *Whose Line*), not laws. Names, mechanics, and win/lose conditions vary by troupe.

*Sources: [improwiki](https://improwiki.com/en/improv-games) · [Improv Archive](https://improvarchive.org/games/party-quirks)*

# Part VI — The Real Physics (loaded in)

*A fair game depends on the components' behavior, not just the rules. Concrete numbers, cited, for the engine to use.*

### Dice — the fair d6
- **Uniform model:** an ideal d6 has P(face) = **1/6 ≈ 16.667%**, independent of prior rolls.
- **Deterministic but effectively random:** a die's flight is Newtonian (a 12-dimensional initial-condition space, per Diaconis), so the outcome is *determined* at release — but the six outcome-regions are so finely interleaved that a sub-millimeter change flips it. The randomness is the roller's inability to reproduce conditions, not indeterminism.
- **Fair vs biased — center of mass:** a die is fair only if geometric center = center of mass. **Drilled-and-painted pips** remove more material from the 6-face (lightest) than the 1-face (heaviest); the heavy face settles down, so cheap dice land **6 up slightly more than 1/6**. **Casino dice** use flush, density-matched pip fills and are transparent so no hidden weight can be inserted.
- **Edges/size/material:** casino dice — **19.05 mm ± 0.0051 mm**, sharp "razor" edges, cellulose acetate; consumer dice — ~16 mm, rounded edges (rounded edges mildly help fairness by preventing edge-balancing, but reduce the crisp chaotic tumbling). A **cocked die** (leaning, not flat) is a no-roll. **Felt** surfaces and a minimum tumble distance defeat "dice control."

**2d6 distribution (backgammon, Monopoly, Catan):** triangular, not uniform — 36 ordered pairs, variable ways per total.

### Coins — flipping is not exactly 50/50
- **Diaconis, Holmes & Montgomery (2007):** a caught coin precesses and lands **~51% same side as it started** — a dynamical bias, true even for a geometrically fair coin.
- **Bartoš et al. (2023), 350,757 flips:** empirically **Pr(same side) = 0.508** (95% CI [0.506, 0.509]) — a **~50.8% same-side bias**, with large person-to-person variation. No heads-vs-tails bias; the effect is same-side.
- **Spinning** a coin is *much* more biased — dominated by the coin's mass asymmetry (embossed relief), e.g. a spun US penny lands **~80% tails**. Landing method matters: catching preserves ~50.8%; free-bouncing adds small edge/friction bias; spinning is large and coin-specific.
- **A coin as a game piece** is therefore a real randomizer — a "d2" with a ~0.8-point same-side bias when caught — and is physically distinguishable by diameter, weight, and edge milling. (This is exactly the physics-layer trap: a coin verified as "legitimate" still doesn't behave like a checker.)

### Rolling/sliding surfaces & fairness
- **Felt vs hard:** felt adds friction, forces tumbling, defeats sliding-throw control; hard/slick surfaces let dice, coins, and checkers slide predictably (exploitable).
- **Level:** an out-of-level table biases both rolling dice and sliding pieces toward the low side. Casinos level and check their tables for this.
- **Fair-roll practice:** use a cup or throw from height; require a minimum tumble (casino craps: dice must hit the textured back wall); both dice must land **flat**, not cocked; prefer a soft, level, high-friction surface.
